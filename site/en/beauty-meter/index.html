Beauty Meter: hover any image and see how heavily it was beautified

This is a Chrome MV3 extension. It turns an image back into pixels and measures three things locally: how much skin micro-texture was smoothed away, how far the skin tone was pushed toward bright and colourless, and how much chroma and contrast were compressed. It then reports a 0~100 beautification score with five explainable dimensions. Everything runs inside your browser — no network, no uploads, no cloud model, no model files at all.

The common case is scoring every image on a feed at a glance; local files go through exactly the same engine and produce the same numbers.

Hover detection only touches <img> elements whose width and height both reach the configured threshold (160px by default), skipping icons and emoji; results are cached per URL, so repeated hovers do not recompute. You can disable hover per site or entirely (see the settings table in Install).

Reading the score: one total plus five dimensions

“Beautification” is not a magic number. A beauty app pipeline is essentially smooth → whiten → grade → re-sharpen, so the detector splits the same way into five dimensions, and every dimension reports its raw evidence (skin micro-texture, skin luma and chroma, chroma standard deviation, and so on).

Score Level Meaning
0–19PristineNo visible beautification
20–39MildSlight smoothing or brightening
40–59ModerateClearly smoothed and whitened
60–79HeavySkin texture largely gone, tone visibly bleached
80–100Extreme“Plastic skin” territory
Dimension Default weight What it measures
Smoothing 40% Whether skin micro-texture was flattened (real photos sit around 5~8 at 512px; heavy smoothing drops to 1~2)
Whitening 20% Whether skin became bright and colourless, plus the share of bright low-chroma pixels
Uniformity 18% Whether skin chroma (Cr/Cb) variation was flattened — blush, shading and blemishes wiped out
Desaturation 11% Whether global and skin saturation were pushed down
Contrast 11% Whether contrast was compressed and highlights clipped

Two markers are reported honestly: confidence (driven by how much credible skin area was found, image size, and whether the relaxed skin rules had to be used; below 50% it is flagged in the UI), and “reference only” — when no credible face skin is found, you get a whole-image reference value instead of a fake conclusion.

How it works: normalise the scale, then look only at skin

There is no model here — just pixel statistics. Two steps are what make it stand up:

  1. Scale normalisation: texture energy is not comparable across image sizes, so every image is first resized (never upscaled) to a 512px working long edge, and every metric is measured there. This is the precondition for the thresholds to mean anything.
  2. Skin mask plus shape gate: a strict YCbCr rule paired with a relaxed rule (for very bright, low-chroma, still-warm “bleached” skin — the warmth requirement is what keeps black-and-white photos from being read as skin). Colour alone is not enough: measured on a food photo, 30% of pixels matched the skin rule. So a shape requirement is added — the largest skin blob must cover ≥4% of the frame, have an aspect ratio within 0.35~1.40 and a bounding-box fill of ≥0.30. Otherwise the verdict is “no credible face skin” and the skin-based dimensions are disabled rather than guessed.

Only then does it measure: 5×5 local standard deviation inside the skin region (micro-texture), the texture ratio between skin and detailed non-skin areas, skin luma and chroma, chroma standard deviation and gradient, global saturation, and RMS contrast with highlight clipping. The five dimensions are weighted and combined, and a sensitivity setting scales how far the score deviates from the midpoint.

The thresholds were not guessed: seven real, unretouched portraits (different skin tones, lighting and eras) pin down the “do no harm” end, a reference beauty pipeline of known strength (0 → 1.0 → extreme) pins down monotonicity and dynamic range, and four counter-examples (landscape, food, warm white marble architecture, black-and-white) pin down the refusal paths. The derivation, the measured data and everything that was fixed or abandoned along the way are documented in the project docs (see Links).

Install and configure

  1. Download the zip above and unzip it (you get a beauty-meter-extension/ directory).
  2. Go to chrome://extensions/ and turn on Developer mode.
  3. Click “Load unpacked” and select that directory (it contains manifest.json directly).
  4. Pin the extension to the toolbar; hover a portrait on any page.

Privacy and permissions

Everything is judged locally: the extension makes no network request for analysis, there is no telemetry, reporting or tracking, and no remote code is loaded (the Manifest V3 default CSP forbids inline and remote scripts).

Permission Why it is needed
storage To store your settings (hover toggle, sensitivity, weights, allowlist)
contextMenus For the right-click entry
<all_urls> (host permission) To read the pixels of the image you asked about. Cross-origin images taint a page canvas, so the extension fetches the bytes in the background and runs fetch → createImageBitmap → OffscreenCanvas. Those bytes never leave your machine and are not written anywhere.

Known limits (worth knowing before you use it)

  • Without credible face skin it cannot judge: landscapes, food, flat colour, black-and-white photos, and bright warm surfaces such as marble, sand and wooden floors are rejected by the shape gate as “no credible face skin” — you get a whole-image reference value or an outright refusal.
  • Full-body shots where the face covers less than 4% are also refused — that is the price of the previous rule: refuse rather than guess.
  • Profiles, strong backlight and heavy stage makeup reduce skin-mask quality, and confidence drops accordingly.
  • Very dark skin (around RGB [80,54,42]) can end up “reference only” for lack of skin pixels, or score conservatively.
  • Naturally very pale or pink-toned skin gets a moderate whitening reading: whitening is inherently relative — without the original there is no “before”, so only the bright, low-chroma bleached look can be detected.
  • Geometric reshaping (bigger eyes, slimmer face) is out of scope: that needs a facial-landmark model, and this extension neither goes online nor ships a model.
  • The score is a likelihood, not a verdict: compression and resizing move it a little, and re-compression by a platform stacks texture loss, which can push the score up.

FAQ

What exactly does it measure?

How much beautification processing an image appears to have gone through — not how attractive anyone is. Specifically: how far skin micro-texture was smoothed away, whether skin was pushed toward bright and colourless, how much skin chroma variation was flattened, and how much saturation and contrast were compressed or clipped.

Is the score accurate?

It holds up for “has this been heavily beautified”: seven real unretouched portraits all score 12~27, and applying a reference beauty pipeline of known strength to the same originals makes the score rise monotonically, reaching 46~76 under strong processing. But it is a probabilistic likelihood: recompression and resizing move it slightly, and naturally very pale skin carries a small whitening reading. Treat it as a clue, not a verdict.

Does it upload my photos?

No. The extension makes no analysis network requests, has no telemetry or reporting, and loads no remote code; pixels are computed in your browser and discarded. The only network activity is you downloading the zip yourself.

Why do some images say “reference only”, and others get refused?

Because when there is no credible face skin, any number would be invented. Black-and-white or near-greyscale images have no colour information, so they are refused with a reason; landscapes, food and flat-colour frames have no credible skin region, so only the whole-image saturation/contrast produce a reference value, with confidence capped and flagged in the UI.

Which browsers are supported, and how do I install it?

Chrome 109+ or a Chromium browser of the same generation (Manifest V3). Installation is “unzip and load”: download the zip, unzip it, open chrome://extensions, enable Developer mode, click “Load unpacked” and select the unzipped directory.

Why isn’t it on the Chrome Web Store?

Listing requires a developer account and a review process; for now the project is distributed as a zip from this site. The upside is that you can read the entire source — the unminified src/ ships inside the package — before installing it.

Can it detect bigger eyes or a slimmer face?

No. Geometric reshaping needs a facial-landmark model, and this extension neither goes online nor ships one; it works purely on pixel statistics. What it is sensitive to instead is the main beauty-pipeline signature: smoothing, whitening and colour compression.
